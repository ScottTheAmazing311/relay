Relay logo concepts

Four marks that say what Relay does, not just how it moves. Judge each on three things: does it read as office moving, does it hold up on dark, and does it survive 16 pixels. All four keep Signal Amber and slate, so any of them drops into the site without touching the rest of the brand.

For reference: the current mark. Clean, but it says "motion" generically; nothing in it is about offices or moving.
A

The moving desk

A desk in side view, under way. The most literal of the four: the object we move is the mark. The amber arrowhead doubles as the direction of travel and the brand accent. Friendly at large sizes, still legible as "table plus arrow" at favicon scale.

B

The stenciled crate

A packing crate with board seams, stenciled with the brand chevrons the way real freight is stenciled with handling marks. Keeps the current logo's equity (the chevrons survive as the stencil) and adds the trade. The strongest as an avatar or app icon, since it is already a filled square.

C

The floor-plan R

The letter R drawn as an architect's floor plan: thick walls, a door gap in the stem, and one amber desk placed inside the bowl. It encodes the part of the job nobody else advertises, reassembly to the space plan, and it is the most distinctive of the four. The trade-off: the idea lands on second look, not first.

D

The hand truck

The tool of the trade, tilted into motion, carrying an amber box stenciled with the chevrons. Instantly reads as "movers" from across the street; the warmest and most blue-collar of the four. Needs the most care at tiny sizes, where the wheel and handle start to merge.
